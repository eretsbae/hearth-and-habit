This hub is the outside of the house: lawn problems, fall and winter yard work, and getting the outside of the house through a freeze. The guides aim for the minimum that keeps the yard healthy, with timing drawn from university extension services for your region rather than a single national calendar.

## Start here

If you read one guide in this section, make it [Fall-to-Spring Yard Calendar: Month-by-Month Lawn Tasks for Cool-Season and Warm-Season Grass](https://www.hearth-habit.com/2026/11/fall-to-spring-yard-calendar-month-by.html). It tells you which month to seed, feed, and mow for your grass type, and the other yard guides here fill in the details.

## How to use this section

- **The lawn looks wrong:** start with the symptom guides: brown patches while watering and grass dying along the sidewalk every winter.
- **Seasonal work:** the month-by-month yard calendar, the fall cleanup checklist, raking versus leaving leaves, how late you can seed, and shutting off the outdoor faucet.
- **Just want it to look fine?** The bare-minimum lawn guide covers the few jobs that matter for people who don't care about lawns.

## What you can expect from these guides

Timing, seed rates, and plant-care rules come from land-grant university extension pages and are cited in each article. Guides are reviewed by peter.bae, and each one lists its sources at the end.
