Caulk peels when the product doesn't match the joint, goes over old caulk or soap film, fills a gap that's too deep, or gets wet before it cures. The tube's label and data sheet cover most of this.

Most guides stop at "silicone for wet areas, latex for trim." That's a start, but it hides the details that decide whether a bead lasts. Two caulks sold for the same tub can differ by days in how soon they tolerate water. Two caulks of the same width can fail differently depending on how deep the joint is. Below are the label lines worth reading, a comparison of three common types, the joint math for gaps that are too big, and the application steps.

## Read the label, not the name

"Kitchen & Bath" on the front of a tube doesn't tell you what's inside. It may be 100% silicone or a siliconized acrylic. The technical data sheet behind the label is where the differences show. Here is how three common products compare, using their manufacturers' published sheets:

| Label line | 100% silicone (GE Advanced Silicone 2 Kitchen & Bath) | Siliconized acrylic (Red Devil Kitchen & Bath) | Painter's caulk (Red Devil) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Water exposure | 30 minutes | No rain or freezing within 72 hours | Protect from water 72 hours |
| Below the waterline | Not limited on the page | Not recommended | Not recommended |
| Paintable | No | Yes | Yes (latex after 2 hours, oil-based after 24) |
| Movement rating | ASTM C920 Class 35 | ASTM C920 Class 25 (white), 12.5 (clear) | ASTM C834 only |
| Application temperature | Not stated on the page | 40°F to 100°F | 40°F to 110°F |
| Backer rod | Gaps over 1/2 in × 1/2 in | Joints deeper than 1/2 in | — |

Three things in that table matter more than the brand:

**"Below the waterline."** Both Red Devil acrylics say they aren't recommended below the waterline. Picture the seam where a tub meets the tile, with water sitting against it during every bath. Use 100% silicone there, even if an acrylic tube shows a bathtub on the front.

**The water wait.** GE says its silicone can take water after 30 minutes. Red Devil asks for 72 hours on its acrylics. That's 72 × 60 = 4,320 minutes, about 144 times as long. If the shower can't be out of service for three days, buy the product with the shorter wait instead of rushing a slow one.

**The ASTM C920 class.** This number is the sealant's movement rating. Red Devil's sheet pairs its Class 25 rating with a stated joint movement of ±25%. For joints that open and close with the seasons, like exterior trim, the C920 class is the number to compare. Basic painter's caulk lists only ASTM C834, with no C920 movement class.

## Match the caulk to the spot

| Where | What to buy | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Tub, shower, sink-to-counter seams that sit wet | 100% silicone | GE's takes water after 30 minutes; acrylics list "not below waterline" |
| Interior trim, baseboards, casing you'll paint | Painter's or siliconized acrylic | Paintable |
| Exterior trim and siding joints | A C920-rated product labeled for exterior use | Joints move with temperature; acrylic needs 40°F and 72 dry hours |
| Gaps deeper than 1/2 in | Backer rod first, then caulk | Controls depth and keeps the bead bonded to two sides |

The University of Arkansas Extension lists the base materials you'll see on labels as acrylic, butyl, oil-base, silicone, and urethane. This guide sticks to the three types in the first table.

## Prep: what "remove old caulk" actually means

All three products here start the same way. Red Devil's directions begin with "remove old caulking." GE lists dirt, grease, moisture, soap residue, and old sealant as things to clear out. Red Devil's painter's caulk sheet adds mildew.

1. **Cut out the old bead.** Score both edges with a utility knife and pull it out. On acrylic or fiberglass tubs, use a plastic scraper so you don't scratch the finish.
2. **Clean off soap scum and mildew.** If you use a bleach solution, use it alone and rinse it away. Poison Control warns that bleach mixed with vinegar, ammonia, or acidic cleaners releases toxic gas.
3. **Let it dry fully.** GE and Red Devil's painter's caulk sheet both call for a dry joint.

If mold covers more than about 10 square feet, the EPA's guide says that warrants a professional rather than a homeowner cleanup. Caulking over mold just hides it.

## Gaps that are too big: backer rod math

A caulk joint works like a rubber band stretched between two sides of the gap. If the caulk also sticks to the back of the gap, it is held on three sides and tears as the joint moves. W. R. Meadows calls this three-sided adhesion failure.

Backer rod fixes both problems. It's a foam rope that you press into the gap before caulking. W. R. Meadows, which makes KOOL-ROD, says sealant won't stick to its rod. It also says the joint depth after the rod goes in should generally be half the width.

**Worked example: a 1/2-inch gap along exterior trim.**

- Width: 1/2 in. Target caulk depth = 1/2 ÷ 2 = 1/4 in.
- Rod: W. R. Meadows' sizing table pairs a 5/8-inch (15.86 mm) rod with a 12 mm (about 1/2 in) joint, so the rod is a bit wider than the gap.
- For a gap about 1/4 inch wide, the same table pairs a 3/8-inch (9.53 mm) rod with a 6 mm joint. The depth works out to 1/4 ÷ 2 = 1/8 in.

Press the rod in evenly with a blunt tool. W. R. Meadows warns against tearing, puncturing, or twisting it, and says to avoid voids that trap air or moisture.

When do you need backer rod? GE says to use it for gaps larger than 1/2 inch by 1/2 inch, and Red Devil says to use it once the joint is deeper than 1/2 inch. Arkansas Extension's advice for drafty door frames: caulking over the molding only treats the symptom. Pull the molding off, seal the gap behind it with non-expanding foam, gap-filler rod, and caulk, and then reinstall the molding.

## How much to buy

University of Arkansas Extension says half a tube is enough for one large window or door. To reseal six windows:

- 6 windows × 1/2 tube = 3 tubes

Buy one more for test beads and mistakes. Red Devil gives its siliconized acrylic a shelf life of at least 1 year at 72°F, so check the date on a spare before using it next season.

## Applying the bead

1. **Check the temperature.** Both Red Devil acrylics list 40°F as the lowest application temperature. Red Devil also warns that cooler, more humid weather slows the cure. Outside with an acrylic, skip any day with rain or freezing forecast within 72 hours.
2. **Tape both sides** of the joint with painter's tape, leaving only the gap exposed.
3. **Cut the tip at 45 degrees.** Arkansas Extension suggests an opening of about 3/8 inch, tested on scrap first. Cut smaller for narrow interior seams, because you can always widen the opening later.
4. **Run the gun at a 45-degree angle** in one steady pass, keeping the bead in contact with both sides of the gap.
5. **Smooth as you go.** Arkansas Extension says to smooth about every 3 feet with a gloved finger, a plastic tool, or a piece of wood, which helps adhesion. Red Devil lists a 10-minute tooling time for its K&B caulk, so don't run a whole room before smoothing.
6. **Pull the tape while the caulk is still wet.**
7. **Wait out the label time** before water, rain, or paint touches it.

## When caulk isn't the fix

If the same seam reopens every few months, or the wall behind it is soft, water may be getting behind it. A failed tub or shower seal upstairs can show up as a [yellow-brown stain on the ceiling below](https://www.hearth-habit.com/2026/09/yellow-brown-ceiling-stain-with-no.html). For deciding whether a hidden problem needs a pro, see [how to tell if a repair is worth calling someone for](https://www.hearth-habit.com/2026/09/how-do-i-know-if-repair-is-actually.html).

Old caulk turns brittle and wears out over time, and Arkansas Extension recommends checking it periodically. A fall walk-around, as part of a [seasonal maintenance checklist](https://www.hearth-habit.com/2026/07/10-home-maintenance-tasks-most-people.html), catches cracked exterior beads before winter.

## FAQ

### Can I put new caulk over old caulk?

Not with these products. Red Devil's directions start with removing old caulking, its painter's caulk sheet calls for a joint free of old caulking, and GE lists old sealant among the things to clear from the joint.

### How long before I can shower?

It depends on the tube. GE's silicone says 30 minutes before water exposure. Red Devil's acrylics say 72 hours. The label wins over any general rule, including this article.

### Why did my new caulk crack down the middle?

The joint moved more than the caulk could stretch. Two likely causes are a low-movement product (painter's caulk on a working exterior joint) and a bead that is too deep or stuck to the back of the gap. Use a C920-rated product with a higher class number, and backer rod in deep gaps.
