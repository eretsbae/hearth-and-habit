A musty smell means moisture is feeding mold, mildew, or bacteria somewhere in the house, and it usually hides in one of six damp, closed-up places: a closet, the basement, the washer, the dishwasher, the fridge, or the towels.

The smell rarely comes from dirt you can see. It comes from surfaces that stay damp and don't get fresh air, which is why a spotless room can still smell like a wet cellar. Air fresheners only mask it. Finding the wet spot is what makes it leave, so start where the smell is strongest and use the table to narrow it down.

| The basement smells musty mostly in summer | Humid outdoor air condensing on cool concrete | Does it get worse after rain (water getting in) or just on muggy days (humidity)? | [Summer basement checklist](https://www.hearth-habit.com/2026/09/why-does-my-basement-smell-musty-only.html) |
| Laundry smells sour right out of the washer | Mildew in the door gasket, detergent drawer, or drain filter | Wash a few towels hot with no softener and smell them the moment the cycle ends | [Musty laundry fix](https://www.hearth-habit.com/2026/09/why-does-my-laundry-still-smell-musty.html) |

## Start With Your Nose, Then Measure the Air

Close interior doors for a few hours, then walk the house and open each closet, cabinet, and appliance one at a time. The first breath when a door opens tells you more than standing in the middle of a room. A smell that's strongest near the floor or in a back corner points to moisture collecting low, where air moves least.

Then measure. An inexpensive digital hygrometer shows relative humidity, and the EPA's advice is to keep it below 60%, ideally between 30% and 50%. If one closet or room reads well above the rest of the house, the problem is local: trapped air or a damp wall. If the whole house sits above 60%, no amount of scrubbing will keep up. The air itself needs drying, typically with a dehumidifier, or with longer air-conditioner run times in summer.

## Closed Spaces: Closets and the Basement

Closets smell first because they're small, sealed, and rarely aired. A closet on an exterior wall runs cooler, so moisture condenses there; one that shares a wall with a bathroom or laundry can pick up dampness through it. Shoes put away wet and cardboard boxes on the floor make it worse. The [closet guide walks through emptying, cleaning, and drying the space](https://www.hearth-habit.com/2026/09/why-does-my-closet-smell-musty-even.html), then adding enough airflow that the smell stays gone.

Basements follow the seasons. Warm summer air carries more moisture, and when it meets concrete kept cool by the surrounding soil, it condenses. That's why opening basement windows on a muggy day can make the smell worse rather than better. If the smell spikes after heavy rain instead of on humid days, suspect water getting in through the walls or poor drainage outside. The [summer basement checklist](https://www.hearth-habit.com/2026/09/why-does-my-basement-smell-musty-only.html) shows how to tell a humidity problem from a leak.

## Machines That Hold Water: Washer, Dishwasher, Fridge

Anything that holds a little water between uses can grow its own smell.

- **Washing machine.** Front-loaders trap water and lint in the rubber door gasket. If clothes come out musty, the machine is the source, not the laundry. Leaving the door and detergent drawer open between loads helps, and the [step-by-step washer cleanup](https://www.hearth-habit.com/2026/09/why-does-my-laundry-still-smell-musty.html) covers the gasket, dispenser, drain filter, and a hot cleaning cycle.
- **Dishwasher.** Running it empty doesn't clean the filter at the bottom of the tub, which is where food and grease collect. [Clean the filter and check the drain](https://www.hearth-habit.com/2026/08/why-does-my-dishwasher-smell-even-after.html) before trying anything else.
- **Refrigerator.** A fridge smell with no obviously spoiled food is typically a spill hiding in a grooved or porous spot. Work through the gasket, drawer tracks, and drip pan [in this order](https://www.hearth-habit.com/2026/08/why-does-my-fridge-smell-but-i-cant.html). Keep in mind that the smell tells you where to clean, not whether food is safe; food safety is judged by time and temperature.

Towels belong on this list too. A towel reused while it's still damp sours quickly, and a bathroom full of them smells musty even right after cleaning. [Washing bath towels by number of uses rather than by the calendar](https://www.hearth-habit.com/2026/08/how-often-should-you-really-wash-bath.html), and hanging them spread out instead of bunched on a hook, keeps that from building up.

## Cleaning It Safely, and Knowing When to Stop

Once the source is found and dried out, scrub hard surfaces with detergent and water and let them dry completely. White vinegar or a diluted bleach solution can go on stubborn mildew spots afterward, but pick one. **Never mix bleach with ammonia or with acids such as vinegar and many toilet-bowl or lime-scale cleaners; the combination releases toxic chlorine or chloramine gas.** If you've used one product, rinse the surface with plain water before using the other, and keep the room ventilated.

Size matters too. The EPA says a homeowner can usually clean mold covering less than about 10 square feet, roughly a 3-by-3-foot patch. Anything bigger, mold inside HVAC ducts, or mold that followed sewage or other contaminated water calls for a professional. So does a smell that lingers after the humidity has stayed below 60% for a while: that usually means water is getting in somewhere you haven't found yet, like behind drywall or under flooring. If you're weighing whether it's time, this guide to [deciding when a repair needs a pro](https://www.hearth-habit.com/2026/09/how-do-i-know-if-repair-is-actually.html) can help you decide.
